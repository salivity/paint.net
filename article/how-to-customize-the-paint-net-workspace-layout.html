How to Customize the Paint.NET Workspace Layout

Paint.NET features a streamlined, modular interface designed for quick navigation and simplicity. While it does not support fully customizable, drag-and-drop ribbon toolbars like some enterprise design suites, users can significantly alter their workspace layout by managing floating utility panels, configuring dynamic toolbars, and adjusting window behavior to fit their workflow. This guide covers how to position, toggle, and reset the essential workspace windows, configure tool settings, and personalize the interface layout.

Managing the Four Utility Windows

The core of the Paint.NET workspace consists of four floating utility windows: Tools, History, Layers, and Colors.

  • Toggling Windows: In the top-right corner of the main window, click the corresponding icons to show or hide any of the four utility windows. Alternatively, use keyboard shortcuts: F5 (Colors), F6 (History), F7 (Layers), and F8 (Tools).
  • Moving Panels: Click and hold the title bar of any floating window to drag it to your preferred location on your screen. These panels can be placed anywhere over the canvas or moved to a secondary monitor to free up your working area.
  • Snapping Windows: When you drag a utility panel close to the edge of the application window or adjacent to another panel, it will automatically snap into place, helping you create a tidy, docked appearance along the borders of your canvas.
  • Dynamic Transparency: If a floating window obscures your canvas while you are drawing, moving your cursor over the covered area will cause the window to become semi-transparent so you can see your work underneath without constantly moving the panel.

Customizing the Tool Options Bar

Directly beneath the standard menu bar is the dynamic Tool Options bar. While the buttons on this bar cannot be manually reordered or removed, the bar automatically adapts to display relevant settings based on the active tool selected in the Tools palette.

When you select a tool (such as the Paintbrush, Text, or Gradient tool), the bar populates with context-specific controls:

  • Adjust brush width, hardness, and stroke styles.
  • Switch selection modes (Replace, Add, Subtract, Intersect, Invert).
  • Toggle anti-aliasing modes and rendering quality.
  • Choose fill styles, tolerance levels, and blending modes.

Any changes made to these parameters remain active for that specific tool during your session, allowing you to establish a predictable, customized behavior for each tool.

Adjusting UI Theme and Interface Elements

To change the overall visual style of your workspace:

  1. Click the Gear icon in the top-right corner to open the Settings dialog.
  2. Select the General tab.
  3. Under the Theme section, choose between Light, Dark, or Use system setting.
  4. You can also customize language settings and determine whether image thumbnails appear in the top tab bar.

Resetting the Workspace Layout

If your floating panels become disorganized, overlap awkwardly, or get pushed off-screen due to display resolution changes, you can instantly restore them to their default positions:

  1. Click Window in the top menu bar.
  2. Select Reset Window Locations.

This command immediately snaps the Tools, History, Layers, and Colors panels back to their original factory positions on the workspace.
