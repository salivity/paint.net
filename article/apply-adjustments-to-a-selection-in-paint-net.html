Apply Adjustments to a Selection in Paint.NET

In Paint.NET, image adjustments and effects are applied globally across an active layer by default. However, you can easily restrict these changes to a specific area of your canvas by using the software's built-in selection tools. By defining an active selection boundary before applying an adjustment, any color change, filter, or correction will exclusively alter the pixels within that boundary while leaving the rest of the layer untouched.

Step 1: Choose the Active Layer

Before making any selections, check the Layers window (press F7 if it is not visible) and click on the layer containing the image or element you want to edit. Adjustments only apply to the currently highlighted layer.

Step 2: Make Your Selection

Choose an appropriate selection tool from the Tools palette (press F5 to open the palette if hidden) or use the keyboard shortcuts:

  • Rectangle Select (S): Draws standard rectangular or square boundaries.
  • Ellipse Select (S): Draws circular or oval boundaries.
  • Lasso Select (S): Allows freehand tracing around irregular shapes.
  • Magic Wand (S): Automatically selects areas with similar colors.

Click and drag over the region you want to edit. Once active, the boundary will be indicated by moving dashed lines, commonly referred to as "marching ants."

Step 3: Refine the Boundary (Optional)

If you need to adjust the chosen area:

  • Hold Ctrl while using a selection tool to add more areas to your current selection.
  • Hold Alt to subtract unwanted portions from the selection.
  • Use the Move Selection tool (the white arrow icon) to reposition or scale the bounding boundary without moving the underlying pixels.

Step 4: Apply the Desired Adjustment

With the selection active, navigate to the top menu bar:

  1. Click on the Adjustments menu.
  2. Select any tool you wish to apply, such as Brightness / Contrast, Hue / Saturation, Curves, or Black and White.
  3. Modify the sliders or settings in the dialog box. You will see a live preview showing that the effect is only being applied inside the dashed boundary.
  4. Click OK to apply the adjustment.

Step 5: Deselect the Area

Once you are finished, clear the selection boundary so you can resume normal editing across the rest of the canvas. You can deselect by:

  • Pressing Ctrl + D on your keyboard.
  • Clicking Edit in the top menu and choosing Deselect.
  • Clicking the Deselect icon in the top toolbar.
