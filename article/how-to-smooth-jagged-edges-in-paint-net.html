How to Smooth Jagged Edges in Paint.NET

Scaling up low-resolution graphics in Paint.NET frequently leads to aliasing, creating harsh, "stair-stepped" pixel edges. This guide explains how to prevent pixelation during the resizing process and how to smooth existing jagged edges using Paint.NET's built-in resampling methods, blur filters, selection tools, and dedicated anti-aliasing plugins.

1. Choose the Correct Resampling Algorithm

If you are currently scaling the image, you can prevent jagged edges before they appear:

  1. Open your image and go to Image > Resize (or press Ctrl + R).
  2. Locate the Resampling dropdown menu.
  3. Switch the algorithm from Nearest Neighbor (which preserves hard, jagged pixels) to Bicubic or Fant.
  4. Click OK. Both Bicubic and Fant automatically interpolate adjacent pixels to create softer, smoother transitions.

2. Apply a Gaussian Blur and Contrast Pass

If the graphic is already scaled and has hard, jagged borders, you can soften the transition using a quick blur-and-sharpen technique:

  1. Isolate the graphic on a transparent layer.
  2. Navigate to Effects > Blurs > Gaussian Blur.
  3. Set the Radius slider to a low value, typically between 1 and 3 pixels, just enough to soften the jagged pixels without losing the overall shape. Click OK.
  4. Go to Adjustments > Brightness / Contrast.
  5. Increase the Contrast slider to tighten the blurred edge, effectively creating a clean, anti-aliased contour.

3. Use the Median Noise Filter

The Median filter rounds off sharp pixel corners while preserving large color blocks, making it ideal for flat-color graphics, logos, and pixel art:

  1. Select the layer containing your jagged graphic.
  2. Go to Effects > Noise > Median.
  3. Set the Radius to 1 or 2 pixels and adjust the Percentile slider (default is 50%).
  4. Preview the canvas to ensure the jagged corners are rounded off without distorting smaller details, then click OK.

4. Smooth Edges Using Selection Feathering

If your subject has jagged edges against a transparent or solid background, smoothing the selection boundary produces a natural contour:

  1. Select the Magic Wand tool (S) and ensure the Anti-aliased selection button in the top toolbar is toggled on.
  2. Click outside the graphic to select the background, or click the graphic itself.
  3. Invert the selection if necessary (Ctrl + I).
  4. Go to Effects > Selection > Feather (available in the popular BoltBait plugin pack for Paint.NET). A radius of 1 or 2 pixels will automatically blend the perimeter into transparency.

5. Use the AA's Assistant Plugin

For graphics with transparent backgrounds, the dedicated AA's Assistant effect (included in the BoltBait plugin pack) is the most efficient solution:

  1. Ensure the graphic is cut out onto a transparent layer.
  2. Navigate to Effects > Object > AA's Assistant.
  3. Adjust the Sharpness and Offset sliders until the pixelated borders smooth out into a clean edge.
  4. Click OK to apply the anti-aliasing directly to the boundaries.
