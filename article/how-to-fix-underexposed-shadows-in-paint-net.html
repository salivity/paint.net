How to Fix Underexposed Shadows in Paint.NET

Underexposed shadows can hide important details and ruin the overall balance of an otherwise great photograph. Paint.NET provides several straightforward, built-in tools that allow you to lift dark areas and recover hidden details without overexposing the highlights. This guide explains the most effective methods to correct underexposed shadows using the Curves adjustment, the Levels tool, and layer blending techniques.

Method 1: The Curves Tool (Recommended)

The Curves adjustment provides the most precise control for brightening shadows while keeping midtones and highlights intact.

  1. Open your image in Paint.NET.
  2. Navigate to the top menu and select Adjustments > Curves.
  3. In the Curves dialogue, ensure the Transfer map is set to Luminosity to prevent unwanted color shifts, or leave it on RGB if you want to adjust overall exposure.
  4. Locate the diagonal line on the grid. The bottom-left corner represents the shadows, the center represents the midtones, and the top-right represents the highlights.
  5. Click near the lower-left portion of the line to create an anchor point, then drag it upward to brighten the dark areas.
  6. Click near the center or upper-right of the line to create a second anchor point, dragging it back toward the diagonal baseline to prevent the brighter areas from blowing out.
  7. Click OK once the shadow detail is visible and balanced.

Method 2: The Levels Tool

The Levels tool is a quick way to remap shadow and midtone values across the entire tonal range.

  1. Go to Adjustments > Levels.
  2. Locate the Input section, which displays a histogram with three sliders: Black (Shadows), Grey (Midtones/Gamma), and White (Highlights).
  3. Drag the middle (Grey) slider to the left to lift the midtones and darker values across the photo.
  4. If the darkest regions appear crushed, drag the bottom-left slider in the Output section slightly to the right to raise the minimum black point.
  5. Monitor the preview in real-time, then click OK to apply the changes.

Method 3: Duplicate Layer with Screen Blending

If you prefer a non-destructive method that preserves raw pixel data, using blend modes can quickly open up harsh shadows.

  1. Open the Layers window (F7).
  2. Click the Duplicate Layer button at the bottom of the window to create a copy of your background image.
  3. Double-click the top layer to open its Layer Properties.
  4. Change the Mode dropdown to Screen. This instantly brightens the dark areas of the image.
  5. Lower the Opacity slider until the shadows look natural and not washed out.
  6. Click OK, then press Ctrl + M to merge the layers if you are finished editing.
