Paint.NET Standard vs Intersect Selection Modes

When working with selections in Paint.NET, understanding how different selection modes interact with your canvas is essential for precise image editing. The primary difference between the standard selection mode (officially called "Replace") and the "Intersect" selection mode lies in how a new selection boundary affects an existing active selection. While the standard mode discards prior selections to create a completely new one, the intersect mode preserves only the overlapping region shared between the existing selection and the new boundary.

Standard Selection Mode (Replace)

The standard selection mode, labeled as "Replace" in the Paint.NET toolbar, is the default behavior for all selection tools (such as the Rectangle Select, Lasso Select, and Ellipse Select).

  • Behavior: Every time you click and drag to define an area, any existing selection on your canvas is instantly cleared and replaced by the new boundary.
  • Result: You are always left with exactly the shape you just drew, regardless of what was previously highlighted.
  • Best Used For: Starting a new selection from scratch or isolating a single, continuous element without needing to combine multiple geometric shapes.

Intersect Selection Mode

The Intersect mode works as a geometric filter that narrows down your active workspace by comparing two boundaries.

  • Behavior: When you draw a new selection while Intersect mode is active, Paint.NET evaluates both the currently selected area and the newly drawn shape. The software then removes everything outside of the overlapping region.
  • Result: Only the area common to both the original selection and the new selection remains active. If your new shape does not overlap the existing selection at all, the entire selection is cleared because there is no common area.
  • Best Used For: Creating complex or non-standard shapes, sculpting intricate boundaries, and cropping specific segments out of an existing selection (such as combining an ellipse and a rectangle to create a rounded wedge).

Key Differences at a Glance

  • Handling Existing Selections: Standard mode removes prior selections entirely; Intersect mode relies on the prior selection to calculate a shared area.
  • Output Size: Standard mode generates a selection equal to your drawn input; Intersect mode always results in a selection that is equal to or smaller than the pre-existing selection.
  • Workflow Role: Standard mode is used for defining base boundaries, whereas Intersect mode is used for refining, cropping, and sculpting complex masks.
