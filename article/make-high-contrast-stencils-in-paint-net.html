Make High-Contrast Stencils in Paint.NET

Creating a high-contrast black and white stencil in Paint.NET is a straightforward process that transforms any standard photograph into a clean, two-tone template suitable for cutting, printing, or screen printing. By stripping away color information, maximizing tonal contrast, and cleaning up stray pixels, you can quickly isolate your subject into pure black and white shapes.

Step 1: Prepare and Desaturate the Image

  1. Open Paint.NET and load your chosen image by going to File > Open.
  2. Remove the color from your image by clicking Adjustments > Black and White (or press Ctrl + Shift + G).

Step 2: Maximize Contrast

To turn grayscale tones into distinct black and white areas:

  1. Go to Adjustments > Brightness / Contrast.
  2. Drag the Contrast slider all the way to 100.
  3. Adjust the Brightness slider left or right until the dark and light areas balance out to display the defining details of your subject.
  4. Click OK.
  5. Repeat this step once more if residual gray pixels remain, pushing the Contrast slider to 100 again to force all pixels into pure black or pure white.

(Alternative Method): Go to Adjustments > Curves, select the Luminosity transfer map, and drag the control points into an abrupt vertical step near the center. This acts as a manual threshold filter.

Step 3: Smooth the Edges (Optional)

High-contrast thresholding can leave jagged pixel edges. To smooth them:

  1. Navigate to Effects > Blurs > Gaussian Blur and set the radius to 1 or 2 pixels.
  2. Go back to Adjustments > Brightness / Contrast and push the Contrast back to 100 to re-harden the softened edges cleanly.

Step 4: Clean Up Stray Pixels

  1. Select the Paintbrush or Pencil tool from the Tools panel.
  2. Set your primary color to pure white (#FFFFFF) to paint over unwanted specks or background clutter.
  3. Switch your primary color to pure black (#000000) to fill in gaps or reinforce weak outlines.

Step 5: Add Stencil Bridges

If you plan to physically cut this stencil out of paper, cardstock, or Mylar:

  1. Identify any "islands"—white areas completely surrounded by black (or vice versa) that would fall out once cut.
  2. Use the Paintbrush tool with the opposite color to draw connecting strips ("bridges") that tether those inner sections to the surrounding material.

Step 6: Save the File

  1. Go to File > Save As.
  2. Choose PNG as the file format to preserve the sharp, uncompressed edges of your final stencil design.
