Tab Key Function for Floating Windows in Paint.NET

In Paint.NET, managing your workspace efficiently is crucial when editing detailed images on screens of any size. This article explains the specific role of the Tab key when dealing with Paint.NET's floating utility windows, detailing how it controls their visibility to maximize canvas space and how it streamlines your editing workflow.

Toggling Floating Window Visibility

The primary function of the Tab key in Paint.NET is to instantly toggle the visibility of all active floating utility windows. These floating panels include:

  • Tools: The primary toolbar containing selection, drawing, and editing tools.
  • History: The list of past actions allowing undo and redo operations.
  • Layers: The panel used to manage, blend, and organize image layers.
  • Colors: The color wheel and palette used for selecting primary and secondary colors.

How It Works

When you press the Tab key once, Paint.NET immediately hides all open floating windows, leaving you with an unobstructed view of the canvas and the primary menu bar at the top.

Pressing the Tab key a second time restores the floating windows back to their previous positions on the screen.

Preserving Workspace State

The Tab shortcut acts as a temporary hide-and-show toggle rather than a reset button. It respects your current workspace configuration:

  • If a specific window (such as History) is already closed before you press Tab, it will remain closed when you press Tab again to bring the other windows back.
  • The shortcut does not reset window coordinates; every panel returns to its exact previous floating location.

Practical Benefits

Using the Tab key provides several practical workflow advantages:

  • Quick Canvas Inspection: You can immediately inspect edge-to-edge details of an image without panels overlapping your artwork.
  • Small Screen Optimization: On laptops or lower-resolution displays where floating windows occupy a large percentage of the screen, the Tab key allows you to alternate between editing tools and a full-screen preview instantly.
  • Distraction-Free Painting: When using brush or pencil tools with preset settings, hiding the panels provides a cleaner, less cluttered interface to focus entirely on the stroke work.
