Is Paint.NET Free for Commercial Use?

Commercial enterprises can legally use Paint.NET completely free of charge. This article explains the licensing terms governing Paint.NET, clarifies the difference between the free installer and the paid Microsoft Store release, and outlines the rules businesses must follow when deploying the software across their workstations.

Paint.NET is licensed as freeware for all standard use cases, including personal, educational, governmental, and commercial environments. The software’s End User License Agreement (EULA) grants users the right to install and use unlimited copies of the application on any number of machines within a business without purchasing a commercial license or paying recurring subscription fees.

While Paint.NET is free, confusion often arises due to the two distinct distribution methods available:

  • The Classic Desktop Installer (Free): Available directly from the official Paint.NET website, this version is 100% free for both individuals and corporate entities. Businesses can download, install, and use this version indefinitely at zero cost.
  • The Microsoft Store Version (Paid): Paint.NET is also offered as a paid app in the Microsoft Store. This purchase is entirely optional and serves primarily as a contribution to support the developer. It offers automated background updates and simplified management via Microsoft accounts, but purchasing it is not a legal requirement for commercial compliance.

Enterprise Deployment and Distribution Rules

Businesses are free to deploy Paint.NET across their internal networks using administrative deployment tools, such as Microsoft Intune, SCCM, or Group Policy, using the standard MSI-based installer.

However, the license does include specific restrictions that enterprises must respect:

  • You may not modify, reverse engineer, or redistribute modified copies of the software.
  • You may not sell, rent, lease, or charge a fee for the software itself.
  • You may not bundle Paint.NET as part of a commercial software package or distribution without explicit permission from the copyright holder.

As long as an organization uses the software internally for work tasks—such as image editing, graphic creation, and asset management—without attempting to resell or repackage the software binary itself, it remains entirely compliant with the Paint.NET license at no cost.
