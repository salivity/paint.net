Pan Tool in Paint.NET: Functions and Shortcuts

This article explains the primary function of the Pan tool (hand scrolling) in Paint.NET, detailing how it facilitates viewport navigation across enlarged or high-resolution images without modifying canvas pixels, along with essential mouse and keyboard shortcuts to maximize workflow efficiency.

The Pan tool in Paint.NET, represented by a hand icon in the Tools window, is designed exclusively for navigating the workspace. When working on detailed projects, high-resolution graphics, or zoomed-in sections of a canvas, the entire image frequently exceeds the boundaries of the viewport. The Pan tool allows you to click and drag the viewing area in any direction to inspect or edit different parts of the image smoothly, serving as a faster, more flexible alternative to the static horizontal and vertical scrollbars.

Because the Pan tool is strictly a navigational utility, it does not alter image data, add layers, or manipulate pixels. You can activate it by clicking the Hand icon in the Tools palette or by pressing the shortcut key H. Once selected, click and drag the canvas using the left mouse button to reposition your view.

For a faster workflow, Paint.NET includes shortcuts that allow panning without switching away from your active tool:

  • The Spacebar Shortcut: While using any other tool (such as the Paintbrush, Pencil, or Magic Wand), press and hold the Spacebar. Your cursor temporarily switches to the Pan hand. Click and drag to reposition the canvas, then release the spacebar to immediately return to your previous tool.
  • Middle Mouse Click: Clicking and holding the middle mouse button (the scroll wheel) anywhere on the canvas lets you pan the view instantly without pressing any keyboard keys.
