How to Create a Solarized Effect in Paint.NET

The solarized photo effect, historically known as the Sabattier effect, partially reverses an image's tone values to produce a distinct, high-contrast, surreal aesthetic. In Paint.NET, you can easily achieve this look using native adjustment tools without installing third-party plugins. This guide provides a straightforward method to recreate the effect using the Curves and Levels adjustments to bend the image's tonal response.

Step 1: Open Your Image and Prepare the Base

Launch Paint.NET and open the photo you want to edit (Ctrl + O). If you want to keep the original photo untouched, duplicate the background layer by pressing Ctrl + Shift + D or clicking the Duplicate Layer icon in the Layers window.

If you want a traditional monochrome solarization, convert the image first by navigating to Adjustments > Black and White (Ctrl + Shift + G). If you prefer a psychedelic, full-color solarized look, skip this step.

Step 2: Apply the Curves Adjustment

The core of the solarization effect relies on reversing certain brightness values using the Curves tool.

  1. Navigate to Adjustments > Curves (Ctrl + Shift + M).
  2. Set the Transfer map dropdown menu to Luminosity (or leave it on RGB if you are working with color and want dramatic color shifts).
  3. Ensure the mode is set to Cubic or Spline rather than Linear for smoother gradients.
  4. Locate the curve line, which starts at the bottom-left (shadows) and ends at the top-right (highlights).
  5. Click and drag the top-right control point all the way to the bottom-right corner.
  6. Click in the middle of the line to add a new control point and drag it straight up to the top of the grid.

This creates an inverted "V" (or mountain) shape. This curve maps midtones to full brightness while forcing both extreme shadows and extreme highlights to become dark, creating the signature solarized inversion. You can adjust the position of the peak to control which tonal ranges invert. Click OK when satisfied.

Step 3: Refine Contrast with Levels

Solarization can sometimes leave the midtones feeling flat or muddy. Use the Levels tool to restore sharp contrast:

  1. Go to Adjustments > Levels (Ctrl + L).
  2. Under the Input section, move the top and bottom slider arrows toward the center to clip the shadows and highlights slightly, boosting overall contrast.
  3. Adjust the middle slider to shift the midtone brightness until the borders around the inverted areas appear sharp and distinct.
  4. Click OK.

Step 4: Optional Layer Blending

To soften the harshness of the effect or create a subtle pseudo-solarization:

  1. Double-click the edited layer in the Layers panel to open Layer Properties.
  2. Experiment with blend modes such as Difference, Overlay, or Exclusion.
  3. Lower the Opacity slider to blend the solarized layer back into the original photo below it for a balanced, stylized finish.
