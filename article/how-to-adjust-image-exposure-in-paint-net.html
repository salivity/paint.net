How to Adjust Image Exposure in Paint.NET

Adjusting image exposure in Paint.NET allows you to correct under- or over-exposed photos, enhance details in shadows, and balance overall lighting. While Paint.NET does not feature a single tool labeled "Exposure," you can achieve precise exposure correction using three native adjustment tools: Brightness / Contrast, Levels, and Curves. This guide details how to use each tool step-by-step to achieve proper exposure for your images.

Method 1: Using Brightness / Contrast (Quick Fix)

The Brightness / Contrast tool provides the fastest way to lighten or darken an image uniformly.

  1. Open your image in Paint.NET.
  2. Navigate to the top menu and select Adjustments > Brightness / Contrast.
  3. Move the Brightness slider to the right to increase exposure, or to the left to decrease it.
  4. Adjust the Contrast slider slightly to ensure the image does not look washed out or overly harsh as the brightness changes.
  5. Click OK to apply the changes.

Method 2: Using Levels (Balanced Tonal Control)

The Levels tool allows you to manipulate dark, midtone, and light values independently, giving you finer control over exposure without clipping details.

  1. Go to Adjustments > Levels.
  2. Look at the Input section on the left side of the window, which shows a histogram with three slider triangles underneath:
    • Left slider (Black point): Drag toward the center to deepen shadows and darken overall tone.
    • Middle slider (Midtones/Gamma): Drag to the left to brighten midtones (increasing exposure), or to the right to darken them.
    • Right slider (White point): Drag toward the center to increase highlight brightness.
  3. Keep the Output sliders at their default positions (0 and 255) unless you want to intentionally reduce overall dynamic range.
  4. Click OK once the lighting appears balanced.

Method 3: Using Curves (Advanced Exposure Control)

The Curves tool offers the most precise method to adjust image exposure across specific tonal ranges.

  1. Select Adjustments > Curves.
  2. Set the dropdown menu at the top to Luminance rather than RGB, as this adjusts light values without shifting color balance.
  3. Locate the diagonal line running across the graph. The bottom-left represents shadows, the middle represents midtones, and the top-right represents highlights.
  4. Click and drag the center of the line upward to brighten the overall exposure. Dragging the center downward darkens the image.
  5. Add additional control points along the curve if you only need to expose specific areas, such as lifting shadow detail while keeping highlights protected.
  6. Click OK to commit the exposure adjustments.
