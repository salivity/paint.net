How to Fix White Balance in Paint.NET

Incorrect white balance can leave your digital photos looking overly warm with an orange tint or excessively cold with a blue cast. Paint.NET provides several straightforward, built-in tools—such as Auto-Level, Levels, and Curves—to quickly neutralize these color casts and restore realistic tones to your images. This guide walks you through the most effective methods to correct color temperature and balance your photos cleanly without complex photo-editing workflows.

Method 1: The Quick Fix with Auto-Level

The fastest way to fix an improper white balance is Paint.NET’s automated correction tool, which attempts to stretch the dark and light points across individual color channels.

  1. Open your image in Paint.NET.
  2. Click on the Adjustments menu at the top.
  3. Select Auto-Level (or press Ctrl + Shift + L on your keyboard).

If the image looks natural, your edit is complete. If the result looks over-saturated or unbalanced, press Ctrl + Z to undo and use a manual method instead.

Method 2: Manual Correction Using Levels

The Levels adjustment provides manual control over the individual Red, Green, and Blue (RGB) color channels, making it ideal for targeted white balance adjustments.

  1. Go to Adjustments > Levels (or press Ctrl + L).
  2. Look at the Input section on the left side of the window.
  3. In the top-left dropdown menu, uncheck the colors you do not wish to alter so you can isolate individual channels (Red, Green, or Blue):
    • For photos that look too blue (cold): Deselect Red and Green, then drag the middle slider of the Blue channel downward to reduce blue, or select Red and slide the middle slider upward to add warmth.
    • For photos that look too orange or yellow (warm): Select the Blue channel and slide the middle slider upward to introduce cool tones, or select Red and slide it downward.
  4. Adjust the middle slider (gamma/midtones) of each channel until neutral objects in the photo (such as white shirts, gray pavement, or clouds) appear free of color tints.
  5. Click OK to apply the changes.

Method 3: Fine-Tuning with Curves

Curves allow for nuanced adjustments to highlights, midtones, and shadows independently.

  1. Go to Adjustments > Curves (or press Ctrl + Shift + M).
  2. Change the dropdown menu from Luminosity to RGB.
  3. Uncheck the channels you want to leave untouched to isolate the problematic color.
  4. Click and drag the diagonal line slightly upward or downward to increase or decrease that specific color in your image.
  5. Focus adjustments on the center of the line to alter the midtones where color casts are most visible.
  6. Click OK once the colors appear balanced.

Method 4: Using a White Balance Plugin

For an eye-dropper approach similar to high-end RAW editors, you can install a community plugin like the White Balance effect from the Ed Harvey or pyrochild plugin packs.

  1. Install the plugin by placing the .dll file into your Paint.NET Effects folder.
  2. Restart Paint.NET and open your photo.
  3. Navigate to Effects > Color > White Balance.
  4. Use the built-in color picker to click on an area of the photo that should be pure white or neutral gray. The plugin will instantly recalculate the entire photo's color temperature to make that selected point neutral.
