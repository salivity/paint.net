How to Share Art and Tutorials on Paint.NET

Contributing tutorials and artwork to the Paint.NET community allows you to showcase your skills, help others learn image editing techniques, and receive constructive feedback. The official Paint.NET forum serves as the primary hub for all community contributions. By creating a forum account, identifying the correct subforum—such as The Pictorium for art or the Tutorial section for guides—and adhering to community formatting and hosting guidelines, you can successfully publish and share your work.

1. Create an Account on the Official Forum

All community-driven showcases and educational guides are managed through the official Paint.NET discussion forum.

  • Visit the forum at forums.getpaint.net.
  • Click Sign Up to create an account.
  • Verify your email address to enable posting privileges.
  • Read the general forum rules to understand the community standards regarding etiquette, content safety, and post formatting.

2. Contributing Artwork to The Pictorium

The forum section dedicated to showcasing finished graphics, digital paintings, and photo manipulations is called The Pictorium.

  • Start a Personal Gallery: Members typically create a single personal gallery thread to hold all their works over time rather than creating a new thread for every individual image.
  • Host Your Images: The forum does not provide unlimited attachment storage. Upload your artwork to an external image host (such as Imgur or Postimages) and embed the image links using standard BBCode tags ([img]URL[/img]).
  • Manage Image Sizes: To keep threads readable, post smaller preview thumbnails that link to full-resolution versions, or place extremely large images inside expandable [spoiler] tags.
  • Attribute Assets: Clearly list any third-party stock photos, textures, or specialized plugins used to produce the piece.

3. Contributing a Tutorial

The Tutorials section is structured to help users replicate specific effects, learn tools, or master complex image compositions.

  • Check for Duplicates: Search the forum to ensure your proposed technique or effect has not already been documented. New submissions should offer unique techniques or significant improvements over older methods.
  • Verify Section Requirements: Note that many tutorials require a minimum level of complexity. Simple one-step actions or standard tool explanations do not qualify as standalone tutorials.
  • Structure Your Guide:
    • Final Outcome: Place an image of the completed result at the very top of your post.
    • Prerequisites: List any required third-party plugins, specific stock photos, or recommended settings before the instructions begin.
    • Step-by-Step Instructions: Break the process down into numbered steps. Write clear descriptions of what settings to adjust (e.g., tolerance, blend modes, layer properties).
    • Visual Aids: Include screenshots for complex steps to help readers follow along easily.

4. Post Maintenance and Community Engagement

  • Fix Broken Links: If an image hosting service drops your images, update the links in your original post to prevent the thread from becoming unusable.
  • Respond to Feedback: Users will often leave comments asking for clarification on tutorials or offering critique on artwork. Engage politely and answer questions when beginners ask for help troubleshooting your steps.
