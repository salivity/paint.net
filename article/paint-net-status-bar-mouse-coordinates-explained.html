Paint.NET Status Bar Mouse Coordinates Explained

The status bar in Paint.NET serves as a vital utility for precision editing by offering real-time feedback on cursor location and canvas geometry. This article covers the exact coordinate details displayed at the bottom of the Paint.NET interface, how those values change during active tool usage, and how to interpret the measurements for pixel-perfect editing.

Real-Time Cursor Position

Located on the right side of the status bar at the bottom of the window, Paint.NET continuously displays the current position of the mouse cursor relative to the canvas.

  • X and Y Coordinates: The position is shown as a pair of numbers corresponding to the horizontal (X) and vertical (Y) axes.
  • Canvas Origin: The coordinate system originates at the top-left corner of the canvas, which represents position (0, 0). Moving the cursor to the right increases the X value, while moving it downward increases the Y value.
  • Canvas Boundaries: Cursor tracking is strictly tied to the document area. When the mouse leaves the active canvas area, coordinate tracking ceases until the cursor returns.

Active Tool and Selection Dimensions

When actively clicking and dragging with specific tools, the coordinate display shifts or expands to provide operational context rather than just a static point:

  • Selection Tools: While dragging a selection (such as the Rectangle Select or Ellipse Select tools), the status bar displays the current width and height of the selection area alongside the origin point.
  • Shape and Line Tools: When drawing shapes, lines, or curves, the status bar displays bounding box dimensions, the overall length of the line, or the angle of rotation in degrees.
  • Move Tools: When using the Move Selected Pixels or Move Selection tools, the status bar indicates the offset distance (delta X and delta Y) from the original position.

Measurement Units

The coordinate values shown in the status bar correspond to the measurement unit selected for the document. Next to the coordinate readout on the status bar, a drop-down menu allows you to toggle between:

  • Pixels: Standard digital display measurement, showing whole integer coordinates.
  • Inches: Physical print measurement, displaying fractional values based on the image's DPI/resolution.
  • Centimeters: Metric print measurement, also displaying decimal values derived from document resolution.
