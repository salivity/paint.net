How to Pixelate and Censor Images in Paint.NET

Censoring sensitive information—such as faces, license plates, or private text—is a standard photo editing task easily accomplished in Paint.NET. This guide provides a straightforward walkthrough on using Paint.NET's built-in selection tools and the Pixelate filter to create clean, effective pixelated censor blocks over any part of an image.

Step 1: Open Your Image

Launch Paint.NET and open the image you want to edit by going to File > Open, or by pressing Ctrl + O and selecting your file.

Step 2: Select the Area to Censor

Choose the appropriate selection tool from the Tools palette on the left:

  • Rectangle Select (S): Ideal for license plates, documents, screen captures, or rectangular text areas.
  • Lasso Select (S): Best for irregular shapes.
  • Ellipse Select (S): Useful for round objects or human faces.

Click and drag over the region containing the sensitive details to create an active selection outline (marching ants). Only the pixels inside this boundary will be altered.

Step 3: Open the Pixelate Filter

With the selection active, navigate to the top menu bar and select Effects > Distort > Pixelate.

Step 4: Adjust the Pixelation Level

A settings window will appear with a Cell size slider:

  • Drag the slider to the right to increase the cell size, making the pixels larger and obscuring more detail.
  • Drag the slider to the left to decrease the cell size for a finer, more subtle effect.
  • Ensure the cell size is large enough that the underlying text or features cannot be deciphered or reconstructed.

Click OK once you are satisfied with the preview.

Step 5: Deselect and Save

Press Ctrl + D (or go to Edit > Deselect) to remove the selection boundary. Finally, save your edited image by navigating to File > Save As and choosing your preferred format (such as PNG or JPEG).
