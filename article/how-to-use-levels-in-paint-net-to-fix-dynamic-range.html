How to Use Levels in Paint.NET to Fix Dynamic Range

Dynamic range issues occur when an image has poor contrast, resulting in washed-out highlights, muddy shadows, or a lack of true blacks and whites. In Paint.NET, the Levels adjustment tool provides precise control over an image's tonal distribution, allowing you to remap shadows, midtones, and highlights. This guide explains how to read the Levels histogram and adjust input and output values to restore contrast and balance dynamic range quickly.

Step 1: Open the Levels Adjustment Tool

Open your image in Paint.NET. Navigate to the top menu and select Adjustments > Levels, or press the keyboard shortcut Ctrl + L. This opens the Levels dialog box displaying histograms and adjustment sliders for input and output values.

Step 2: Read the Histogram

The Levels window displays two main columns: Input on the left and Output on the right.

The graph in the Input section is a histogram representing the tonal distribution of the image:

  • The bottom of the graph represents pure black (shadows).
  • The top of the graph represents pure white (highlights).
  • The middle represents midtones (gamma).

A dynamic range issue is visible when there are flat, empty gaps at the top or bottom of the histogram graph, indicating that the photo lacks true highlights or deep shadows.

Step 3: Adjust the Black and White Points (Input Sliders)

To expand the dynamic range and restore full contrast:

  1. Set the Black Point: Locate the bottom slider on the Input side. Drag it upward toward the center until it aligns with the point where the histogram data actually begins. This sets the darkest pixels in the image to true black.
  2. Set the White Point: Locate the top slider on the Input side. Drag it downward toward the center until it meets the point where the histogram data ends. This remaps the brightest pixels to pure white.

Step 4: Balance the Midtones

Once the extreme darks and lights are anchored, adjust the middle Input slider (gamma).

  • Drag the middle slider upward to brighten overall midtone exposure without blowing out highlights.
  • Drag it downward to darken midtones and add density without crushing shadows.

Step 5: Check Output Sliders and Apply

Leave the Output sliders at their default positions (0 for black, 255 for white) unless you need to intentionally compress the range to prevent harsh clipping.

If you prefer an automated baseline, click the Auto button in the dialog box, and then manually refine the sliders. Once the image shows balanced contrast and natural tonal depth, click OK to commit the changes.
