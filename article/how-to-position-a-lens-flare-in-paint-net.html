How to Position a Lens Flare in Paint.NET

Positioning a lens flare in Paint.NET can be accomplished either by using a dedicated lens flare plugin or by manipulating a flare overlay image on a separate layer. This guide details both methods, walking you through the steps to accurately align the light source with your background image using coordinate controls and layer transformation tools.

Method 1: Positioning an Overlay Lens Flare (No Plugins Required)

Using a transparent or black-background lens flare image on an independent layer gives you complete manual control over its placement, angle, and scale.

  1. Add a New Layer: Open your background image. In the Layers window, click the Add New Layer button (or press Ctrl + Shift + N).
  2. Insert the Flare: Open your lens flare image, copy it (Ctrl + C), select your newly created layer, and paste it (Ctrl + V).
  3. Change the Blend Mode: If the flare has a solid black background, double-click the flare's layer to open Layer Properties. Set the Blending Mode to Screen or Additive. This will remove the black background, leaving only the light visible.
  4. Position the Flare: Select the Move Selected Pixels tool (the blue arrow in the Tools palette, or press M). Click and drag the flare to place the center of the light burst directly over your desired light source.
  5. Scale and Rotate: Click and drag the corner nubs to resize the flare. To rotate the light rays or streaks, right-click and drag outside the selection boundary.

Method 2: Positioning with a Lens Flare Plugin

If you have an effect plugin installed (such as those included in community plugin packs like Pyrochild's or Ed Harvey's effects), you can position the flare directly within the effect settings window.

  1. Prepare the Layer: Always create a new blank layer above your photo before generating an effect so you can edit it independently later.
  2. Open the Effect: Go to the top menu, select Effects, navigate to either Render or Photo (depending on your installed plugin), and select Lens Flare.
  3. Use the Interactive Crosshair: Most Paint.NET flare plugins display a thumbnail preview of your canvas containing a crosshair or target icon. Click anywhere on this thumbnail preview to instantly snap the center of the flare to that location.
  4. Adjust Numerical Coordinates: If precise placement is required, use the Center X and Center Y sliders or numeric input fields:
    • Center X: Controls horizontal placement (lower values move left, higher values move right).
    • Center Y: Controls vertical placement (lower values move up, higher values move down).
  5. Finalize and Fine-Tune: Adjust the flare's radius, brightness, and angle to match your scene's perspective, then click OK. Because the flare is on its own layer, you can still use the Move Selected Pixels tool (M) on the canvas to nudge its final position into place.
