Create a Split-Tone Duotone in Paint.NET

This guide explains how to transform a standard portrait into an artistic, stylized split-tone duotone image using Paint.NET. You will learn how to prepare your base image in black and white, maximize contrast, create dedicated color cast layers for highlights and shadows, and use blend modes to achieve a balanced, high-impact finish.

Step 1: Prepare the Base Portrait

  1. Open your portrait in Paint.NET.
  2. Convert the image to black and white by navigating to Adjustments > Desaturate (or press Ctrl + Shift + G).
  3. Increase the dynamic range to make the duotone colors stand out. Go to Adjustments > Brightness / Contrast or Adjustments > Curves. Increase contrast so that shadows are deep and highlights are bright without losing critical facial details.

Step 2: Add the Shadow Tone

  1. Create a new layer above your black-and-white image by clicking the Add New Layer button in the Layers window (or press Ctrl + Shift + N).
  2. Double-click the new layer and rename it "Shadows."
  3. Select the Paint Bucket tool (F) and choose your desired shadow color from the Colors palette (typically a cool shade like navy blue, deep purple, or dark teal).
  4. Click inside the canvas to fill the "Shadows" layer completely with this color.
  5. Press F4 to open Layer Properties. Change the Blend Mode to Multiply.
  6. Adjust the layer's Opacity slider (typically between 120 and 200) so the dark tones take on the color without completely crushing the details.

Step 3: Add the Highlight Tone

  1. Create another new layer above the "Shadows" layer and rename it "Highlights."
  2. Choose a contrasting warm or bright color (such as soft yellow, vibrant orange, or warm pink).
  3. Fill this layer completely with the chosen highlight color using the Paint Bucket tool.
  4. Press F4 to open Layer Properties for the "Highlights" layer. Set the Blend Mode to Screen or Lighten.
  5. Lower the Opacity slider until the highlights look naturally tinted rather than completely blown out.

Step 4: Fine-Tune the Balance

  1. Select the original background layer. If the colors appear muddy, open Adjustments > Levels or Adjustments > Curves to adjust brightness and contrast beneath the color layers in real time.
  2. Tweak the opacity of both the "Shadows" and "Highlights" layers independently until you achieve the desired split-toning harmony.
  3. Once satisfied, go to Image > Flatten (or press Ctrl + Shift + F) to merge the layers into a single finished portrait.
