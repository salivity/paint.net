Render Fractal Landscapes with Paint.NET

Paint.NET provides native procedural tools capable of generating complex terrain textures without the need for third-party plugins. By leveraging the built-in Clouds render filter in conjunction with adjustment tools and stylization effects, you can simulate realistic, fractal-based landscapes and elevation maps directly on your canvas. This guide outlines the exact steps to generate, texture, and sculpt fractal-based terrain using Paint.NET's default toolset.

Step 1: Generate the Base Fractal Heightmap

  1. Open Paint.NET and create a new image (e.g., 1920x1080 pixels).
  2. Reset your primary and secondary palette colors to the default black and white by pressing D.
  3. Navigate to Effects > Render > Clouds.
  4. In the Clouds dialog, adjust the Scale slider to set the zoom level of your terrain. A scale between 250 and 500 creates broad mountain ranges, while lower values produce tighter, jagged hills.
  5. Adjust the Roughness slider to control the fractal complexity. Higher roughness increases rocky detail, while lower values generate rolling, smooth hills.
  6. Click OK to render the noise pattern, which serves as your terrain heightmap.

Step 2: Shape Elevation with Curves

  1. Navigate to Adjustments > Curves.
  2. Select the Luminosity transfer mode.
  3. Pull down the lower-left section of the curve to create flatter lowlands and ocean basins (pure black).
  4. Pull up the upper-right section to compress the highest peaks into sharper snowlines (pure white).
  5. Click OK to apply the elevation contrast.

Step 3: Simulate 3D Lighting and Relief

  1. In the Layers panel, click Duplicate Layer to create a copy of your heightmap.
  2. Ensure the top layer is selected, then navigate to Effects > Stylize > Relief.
  3. Adjust the Angle slider to establish a consistent light source across your landscape. A 45-degree angle typically yields natural-looking shadows.
  4. Set the blend mode of this top layer to Overlay by double-clicking the layer in the Layers window. This projects directional light and shadow onto the original heightmap beneath it, giving the fractal pattern distinct ridges and valleys.

Step 4: Apply Landscape Colorization

  1. Create a new layer above the existing layers and name it "Colors".
  2. Set the "Colors" layer blend mode to Multiply or Color.
  3. Use the Gradient tool (set to Linear or Radial) to lay down horizontal or regional color zones:
    • Deep blue for the lowest values (water bodies).
    • Sandy tan or lush green for intermediate elevations (shores and plains).
    • Rocky brown or dark gray for higher elevations (slopes).
    • Off-white for the highest elevations (snow-capped peaks).
  4. Alternatively, merge all existing terrain layers down into a single layer and use Adjustments > Levels or Adjustments > Hue / Saturation to tint the grayscale values into natural earthy tones.

Step 5: Add Atmospheric Depth

  1. Create a final layer on top of all others for atmospheric fog.
  2. Select the Gradient tool, set the primary color to white and the secondary color to fully transparent white.
  3. Draw a linear gradient from the bottom to the top (or top to bottom, depending on your horizon perspective) to simulate atmospheric haze over distant terrain features.
  4. Reduce the layer opacity to between 20% and 40% to soften the landscape contours and complete the scene.
