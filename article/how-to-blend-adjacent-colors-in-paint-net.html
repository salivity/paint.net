How to Blend Adjacent Colors in Paint.NET

Blending adjacent colors directly on the canvas in Paint.NET requires either a dedicated plugin or built-in workarounds, as the base software does not feature a native interactive smudge brush. This article explains the most effective tool for dynamic color blending—the Smudge plugin—alongside the primary native methods available to blend colors without third-party add-ons.

The Smudge Tool (Pyrochild Plugin)

To blend adjacent colors dynamically on the canvas in the same manner as Photoshop or Procreate, you must use the Smudge tool, available through the popular Pyrochild plugin pack.

  1. Install the Pyrochild plugin pack by placing the .dll files into your Paint.NET Effects folder.
  2. Open Paint.NET and navigate to Effects > Tools > Smudge.
  3. A separate working window will appear with brush settings, such as brush size and strength.
  4. Click and drag across the border of your adjacent colors to smear and mix them directly.
  5. Click OK to apply the blended result to your canvas.

Native Alternative: Selection and Gaussian Blur

If you cannot install plugins, the most precise native method to blend adjacent colors directly on the canvas involves combining selection tools with the Gaussian Blur effect:

  1. Choose the Lasso Select tool or Rectangle Select tool from the toolbar.
  2. Draw a selection box over the boundary where the two adjacent colors meet.
  3. Go to the top menu and select Effects > Blurs > Gaussian Blur.
  4. Adjust the Radius slider until the hard seam softens into a smooth transition.
  5. Click OK and press Ctrl + D to deselect the area.

Native Alternative: The Gradient Tool

For creating a completely new blend between two colors rather than modifying existing brush strokes, use the Gradient tool:

  1. Set your Primary color to the first color and your Secondary color to the second color in the Colors palette.
  2. Select the Gradient tool (shortcut: G).
  3. Set the gradient type in the top toolbar to Linear.
  4. Click and drag across the canvas from one color zone to the next to generate a smooth, mathematically blended transition directly over the surface.
