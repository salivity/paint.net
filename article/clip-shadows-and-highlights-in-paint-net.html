Clip Shadows and Highlights in Paint.NET

Clipping shadows and highlights adjusts the dynamic range of an image by forcing the darkest and lightest pixels into pure black and pure white, boosting overall contrast. In Paint.NET, this process is accomplished using the Levels adjustment tool, which displays a real-time histogram of your image's tonal distribution. This guide explains how to read that histogram and adjust the input thresholds to intentionally clip your shadows and highlights.

Step 1: Open the Levels Adjustment Tool

  1. Open your image in Paint.NET.
  2. Navigate to the top menu bar and click on Adjustments.
  3. Select Levels from the dropdown menu (or press Ctrl + L on your keyboard).

Step 2: Understand the Histogram Interface

The Levels dialog box displays two main sections: Input on the left and Output on the right.

  • The Input section features a histogram showing the distribution of pixels from darkest (bottom) to lightest (top).
  • There are two primary sliders next to the Input histogram: the bottom slider controls the black point (shadows), and the top slider controls the white point (highlights).
  • The middle slider controls the gamma (midtones).

Step 3: Clip the Shadows

Clipping shadows means taking dark gray pixels and converting them entirely to pure black (RGB value 0, 0, 0), eliminating detail in the darkest areas.

  1. Locate the bottom slider on the Input side.
  2. Click and drag this slider upward into the histogram data.
  3. As the slider moves past the base of the histogram graph, all pixel values falling below the slider's current position are clipped to pure black.
  4. Stop dragging when you achieve the desired depth and contrast in the dark regions of the image.

Step 4: Clip the Highlights

Clipping highlights means taking light gray pixels and converting them entirely to pure white (RGB value 255, 255, 255), blowing out details in the brightest areas.

  1. Locate the top slider on the Input side.
  2. Click and drag this slider downward into the histogram data.
  3. Any pixel values falling above this slider's position will be forced to absolute white.
  4. Stop dragging once the highlights have reached your preferred level of brightness.

Step 5: Fine-Tune and Apply

  • Adjust the middle slider between the shadow and highlight controls to balance the midtone exposure if clipping has made the overall composition too dark or too light.
  • Ensure the Output sliders remain at their default values (0 at the bottom, 255 at the top), as moving these would compress the tonal range rather than clip it.
  • Click OK to apply the clipped tonal adjustments to your layer.
