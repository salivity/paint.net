How to Add Grain in Paint.NET Using Add Noise

Adding random grain to an image is a great way to simulate vintage film, reduce digital banding, or add tactile texture to digital artwork. This guide explains how to use the built-in Add Noise effect in Paint.NET to create and customize a realistic grain overlay, control its intensity, and blend it seamlessly with your underlying image.

Step 1: Open Your Image and Create a Grain Layer

  1. Launch Paint.NET and open the image you want to edit.
  2. In the Layers window (press F7 if it is not visible), click the Add New Layer button at the bottom left.
  3. Double-click this new layer, rename it to "Grain," and click OK. Working on a separate layer allows you to adjust the grain non-destructively without altering your base image.

Step 2: Fill the Layer

  1. Select the Paint Bucket tool from the toolbar (press F).
  2. Open the Colors window (press F8), click More, and set the RGB values to 128, 128, 128 (a neutral 50% gray).
  3. Click anywhere on the canvas to fill the "Grain" layer with neutral gray.

Step 3: Apply the Add Noise Effect

  1. With the "Grain" layer selected, go to the top menu and select Effects > Noise > Add Noise.
  2. Adjust the sliders in the configuration dialog:
    • Intensity: Controls the visibility and harshness of the grain. A value between 30 and 70 typically provides a natural texture.
    • Color Saturation: Drag this slider all the way to 0 for realistic, monochromatic film grain. Leaving it higher introduces multi-colored digital sensor noise.
    • Coverage: Determines how densely packed the noise specks are. Setting this near 100% creates an even distribution of grain.
  3. Click OK to apply the effect.

Step 4: Blend the Grain with the Image

  1. In the Layers window, select the "Grain" layer and click the Properties button (the wrench icon) at the bottom right.
  2. Change the Mode dropdown to Overlay or Soft Light. "Soft Light" produces a subtle, gentler grain, while "Overlay" produces a sharper, higher-contrast look.
  3. Adjust the Opacity slider to fine-tune the strength of the grain until it matches your desired aesthetic (typically between 30 and 80).
  4. Click OK to finalize the settings.

Optional: Soften the Grain

If the noise appears too sharp or pixelated, go to Effects > Blurs > Gaussian Blur while on the "Grain" layer. Set the radius to 1 or 2 pixels to smooth out the texture and produce a softer film-style effect.
