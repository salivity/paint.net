Compare Design Versions with Layers in Paint.NET

Comparing different design iterations is an essential part of the creative process, and Paint.NET makes this straightforward through its layer management tools. By placing each variation on an independent layer, you can use layer visibility toggles to switch between concepts instantly without altering the original artwork. This guide explains how to organize your design variants on separate layers and use the visibility checkboxes to perform direct, side-by-side or alternating comparisons.

1. Organize Designs on Separate Layers

To compare variations, each distinct design must live on its own layer above any shared background elements.

  1. Open your project in Paint.NET.
  2. If the Layers window is not visible, press F7 or click the Layers icon in the top-right toolbar to open it.
  3. Click the Add New Layer button (the green plus icon) at the bottom of the Layers window for each design variation.
  4. Double-click each layer to open Layer Properties, then rename the layer to identify the version (e.g., "Version A - Blue Header," "Version B - Red Header").

Keep any shared, unedited elements—such as a common background or persistent UI frames—on the bottom-most layer.

2. Use the Visibility Checkboxes to Compare

Every layer in the Layers window has a checkbox located on its right side. This checkbox controls the layer's visibility on the canvas.

  • Toggle Off: Uncheck the box to hide the layer completely from view.
  • Toggle On: Check the box to make the layer visible again.

To compare two versions:

  1. Place Version B directly above Version A in the layer stack.
  2. Ensure Version A's visibility checkbox is checked.
  3. Rapidly click the checkbox for Version B on and off.

When Version B is checked, it displays over Version A. When unchecked, Version B disappears, instantly revealing Version A beneath it. This rapid toggling creates a "flicker comparison," allowing your eyes to immediately detect subtle differences in layout, alignment, color, and typography.

3. Compare Using Blend Modes and Opacity

For deeper analysis beyond simple on/off switching:

  1. Double-click the top variation layer to open its Layer Properties.
  2. Lower the Opacity slider to around 128 (50%). This overlays the two versions semi-transparently, making it easy to check sizing differences and pixel alignment.
  3. Alternatively, change the Blending Mode to Difference. Identical pixels will appear completely black, while any differences between the two designs will light up in color, highlighting precisely where the layouts diverge.
  4. Set the mode back to Normal and opacity to 255 once the alignment check is complete.
