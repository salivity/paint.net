Paint.NET Polygon Selection Tool Purpose and Use

This article explores the purpose and practical application of the Polygon Selection tool in Paint.NET. It explains how this specialized feature functions, why it is essential for isolating specific parts of an image, and the scenarios where it outperforms basic rectangular, elliptical, or freeform selection options.

The primary purpose of the Polygon Selection tool (often referred to as the polygonal lasso) is to create custom, straight-edged selection boundaries. Unlike standard geometric tools that restrict you to rectangles or ovals, the polygon tool allows you to map out arbitrary shapes by plotting individual anchor points, connecting them with straight line segments to form a closed polygonal perimeter.

This tool solves the common problem of user instability encountered with the standard Lasso tool. Drawing a complex shape freehand often results in shaky, jagged edges due to natural hand movement. The Polygon Selection tool eliminates hand jitter entirely by drawing perfectly straight lines between defined click points, delivering sharp, predictable borders.

The Polygon Selection tool is most effective when isolating man-made or geometric subjects, including:

  • Architecture and Buildings: Tracing walls, roofs, windows, and structural lines.
  • Product Photography: Cutting out boxed items, screens, electronics, and angular packaging.
  • Perspective Cutouts: Selecting surfaces angled away from the camera that form trapezoids or irregular quadrilaterals rather than simple rectangles.

To use the tool, click on the canvas to place your initial anchor point, move your cursor to the next edge, and click again. Repeat this process around the perimeter of the subject. The selection finalizes once you click back on the starting point or double-click to automatically close the loop.

Like other selection tools in Paint.NET, the Polygon Selection tool supports multiple selection modes: Replace, Add (Union), Subtract, Intersect, and Invert. This allows you to combine straight-edged shapes, trim excess areas from existing selections, or refine complex masks with high precision.
