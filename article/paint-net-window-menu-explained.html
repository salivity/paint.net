Paint.NET Window Menu Explained

The Window menu in Paint.NET provides centralized control over the application's workspace layout, floating utility panels, and open documents. This guide outlines the specific options and information displayed in this menu, explaining how each setting functions to help you manage your digital workspace efficiently.

Utility Panel Toggles

At the top of the Window menu, you will find toggles for the four primary floating utility windows in Paint.NET. A checkmark appears next to any panel that is currently visible on the screen. Clicking an item toggles its visibility on or off:

  • Tools (F5): Toggles the main toolbar containing drawing, selection, and editing tools.
  • History (F6): Opens or closes the panel tracking your project actions, allowing you to undo or redo previous edits.
  • Layers (F7): Displays the layer management panel used to create, delete, merge, and adjust project layers.
  • Colors (F8): Toggles the color palette and color picker window used for choosing primary and secondary drawing colors.

Layout Management

Directly below the panel toggles, the Window menu includes workspace arrangement options:

  • Reset Window Locations: Restores the Tools, History, Layers, and Colors panels to their default docked positions. This is particularly useful if panels are accidentally moved off-screen, hidden behind secondary monitors, or misplaced after changing display resolutions.

Open Document List

The lower section of the Window menu displays a dynamic list of all images currently open in Paint.NET:

  • Active Canvas Indicator: The document currently in focus has a checkmark displayed beside its filename.
  • Direct Image Switching: Clicking any listed filename immediately brings that image to the foreground as the active editing canvas.
  • Document Status: Files are listed by their saved names, making it easy to identify specific assets when working on multiple projects simultaneously.
